Changing Places, Changing Perspectives: My Research Visit to Surrey

Created on October 28, 2026 by Davide Iannelli

2026   ·   research-visit   academic-collaboration   ·   academic-life

Some journeys begin as a change of place and end up as a change of perspective. In September 2026, I joined the EASy Group at the University of Surrey as a Research Engineer, visiting from the University of Naples Federico II, where I am currently finishing my PhD. These weeks have been an opportunity to step into a new academic environment, exchange ideas, and look at my own research with different eyes.

Fig. 1. A new academic home at the University of Surrey.

My research

Traditional car-following (CF) models assume perfect human perception and decision-making. Human factors (HF) have been embedded into these models to overcome this limitation, but to what extent do HF-augmented models actually improve their explanatory power, rather than merely adding complexity?

Together with Prof. Vincenzo Punzo, Prof. Marcello Montanino, and Dr. Andrea Saltelli, I developed a methodology to answer this question. It is rooted in Popper’s concept of “risky tests” and model verisimilitude, and was recently published in Transportation Research Part B: Methodological.

At Surrey, I focused on what car-following models still fail to capture. Building on the analysis of calibration residuals from naturalistic trajectory data, I am developing a new HF layer to account for perception errors, which will be put to the test using the very methodology we proposed.

Fig. 2. With the EASy Group at the University of Surrey.

Life in the UK

Beyond the academic side, there is something special about stepping into a new place, embracing its rhythms, and letting it surprise you. Guildford gave me quiet streets, green hills all around, and the kind of peace that makes it easy to think.

And then, London calling! Just a short train ride away, a city that reveals two very different faces. One is loud, restless, and full of colour, where countless cultures, languages, and stories coexist in a single place. The other is surprisingly calm, shaped by quiet parks and peaceful corners where the city seems to slow down.

Fig. 3. Home of The Arsenal, and a little bit mine too.

Thank you

A heartfelt thank you to Dr. Yinglong He and to Connie, Eleanor, Haochen, and Scott for making me feel at home from the very beginning.

I’m leaving Surrey with new ideas, new friends, and connections that, I hope, will go far beyond the time spent here.
